# Known limitations, v0 (launch, 9 Nov 2026)

Measured on a HyperEVM fork against the real Vault V2 bytecode (`contracts/test/Recognition.t.sol`).

## L1. Senior cover is recognised over days, not at once
Measured on the fork with `coverInProgress()` exposed for the front end.
Vault V2 caps how fast its share price may rise: `maxRate`, and the protocol maximum is 200% APR (`MAX_MAX_RATE`), which we use. That is 0.548% of total assets per day. A 2% cover payment therefore closes 29% of the gap after 1 day, 58% after 2, 87% after 3, 100% after 4 (measured). The transfer itself is counted immediately in `realAssets`; only the price moves gradually. Normal yield is far below the cap and is unaffected.

What a senior holder sees after a loss: the price drops at once (V2 marks losses immediately, except a Felix bad debt, which V2 never marks: the cover sees only the growth of Felix's booked `lostAssets` since the baseline, see L11), stays down until junior's next `settle()` (up to 7 days), then recovers over up to 3.7 days. Anyone who withdraws inside that window takes the unrecognised part of the loss; the money they leave behind goes to the holders who stayed. Nobody outside senior and junior gains.

Front end, v0: when junior reports a cover in progress, the senior panel shows "Cover in progress: n% recognised, whole in about h hours" and says plainly that withdrawing before then realises the gap. Read from `JuniorVault.coverInProgress()` (remaining USDC) and senior's `maxRate`.

Smallest fix, v1: the up-to-7-day wait for `settle()` is the larger part of the window and is ours to remove. Make `cover()` a permissionless function callable any time (same baseline logic, its own timestamp for the fee drift), and have the Rotator call it hourly when senior's price is below the fee-adjusted baseline. About 15 lines in JuniorVault, no new accounting. Window becomes about 1 hour plus up to 3.7 days. The 3.7 days cannot be removed without leaving Vault V2's accounting; a periphery that tops up senior withdrawers from junior during the window is possible but moves value between senior holders and is not recommended.

## L2. Total deposit cap on senior is soft
V2 has per-id caps, no single total cap. Deposits enter the Felix (liquidity) adapter, whose absolute cap is the launch cap, so the hard bound holds only while the Rotator has not moved funds out of Felix. Worst case about 2.2x the stated cap. Exact fix: a receive-shares gate contract (custom, small) or the Rotator shrinking the Felix cap as it deploys (sentinel role, instant). v1.

## L3. Stake is quoted at an owner-set rate
No ORPH price source exists on launch day. `stakePerAsset` is set by the owner and can be changed; existing stakes are never re-quoted.

## L4. Senior shares deposited since the last sync are uncovered until the next sync
Cover is billed only for senior shares that existed at the last `syncSenior()` (otherwise anyone could buy senior after a loss and have junior pay them). The keeper syncs hourly and every junior deposit syncs, so the uncovered window is about an hour. Double payment under maxRate lag is fixed (real-asset measurement).
After an uncovered loss (a settle that could not pay what was owed, see L9) the window stays open: while that debt is outstanding `syncSenior()` only observes and never moves the baseline, so senior shares bought after the loss are not covered until the settle that clears the debt, after which the first loss-free sync covers them.

## L5. Junior exits need idle USDC
`claimExit` pays only from the contract's USDC balance. The Rotator must return reserved assets to idle before maturities; if it does not, claims revert until it does. No user funds are at risk, only delayed.

## L6. Agent allowlist is unaudited custom code
About 40 lines. Covered by tests for the Morpho and ERC-4626 shapes used at launch. Zodiac Roles (audited) is the week-two replacement.

## L7. Portfolio principal lags up to an hour
Principal comes from the hourly index; a deposit made in the last hour shows under value but not yet under principal, so earned briefly reads negative by that amount. The page says so.

## L8. Senior APY shows n/a until Morpho indexes the vault
The Morpho API does not list the new Vault V2 yet; ask Morpho to index 0x3F7382bd8BFC6C548CAa2D7B4dfCe628B12A9759. Until then the senior card shows n/a with a note pointing at the Felix rate. The junior projected APY stays n/a until that input exists.

## L9. Junior cover is paid from idle only; an unpaid remainder is an explicit debt
`_coverSenior` pays from junior's idle USDC (net of assets reserved for priced exits); it does not pull from venues. The keeper's settle-day recall exists for this reason. At every settle the amount owed is the new shortfall plus the carried debt, `carryPerShare * exposed` (event `Cover(owed, paid)`); it is paid up to idle and what is still unpaid becomes the new `carryPerShare` (event `CoverShortfallCarried(unpaid)`), per exposed senior share, so exits shrink it and buyers after the loss cannot enlarge it (the baseline supply stays at the exposed shares). Senior's baseline price is the plain post-payment price, so a week of senior yield does not net the debt. Junior's NAV is net of the debt (`carryOwed()`) in `totalAssets()`, the deposit cap, deposit pricing and the settle's frozen price, so a junior exit priced at a partial-cover settle bears its share, and a deposit while a debt is outstanding mints at the debt-net price. With junior idle at 0 the settle still goes through, paying 0 and carrying all of it, every week, until idle returns (measured: carried twice at the same amount, billed once on return). A new shortfall of less than 1 ppm of senior's price, or a carry that rounds down to 0 per share, is not billed: the 1 ppm guard applies to the shortfall, and `carryPerShare` is floored. The carried debt is paid down only by junior's idle USDC at the settle, but senior's own revenue is not separated from it: FeeRouter's USDC sent to senior, donations and adapter-split gains raise senior's price and count towards the baseline like any recovery, so in a week with such inflows the next shortfall can be zero while the carry is still billed in full (senior revenue is junior's debt service). Minted ERC-4626 positions in junior should stay small relative to the buffer until a permissionless cover path exists (v1). If junior's NAV net of the debt is 0 (the debt exceeds what junior holds), shares price at 0 and deposits revert until the debt clears; this is the existing wiped-junior behaviour.

## L10. An exit and a buy between two observations of senior's supply are still billed as one exposure
Cover is billed on `exposed = min(senior supply now, supply at the baseline, lowest supply observed since the baseline)`. Senior shares that left after a loss and were replaced by a buyer are not billed again only if `observeSenior()` (also run by `syncSenior()`, `deposit()` and the settle's own supply read) saw the lower supply between the exit and the buy. The keeper is expected to call `observeSenior()` hourly (keeper change: a later task); an exit and a buy that both fall inside the same gap stay invisible. Measured on the fork (100k senior, 2k loss, half of senior exits, a buyer enters with the same USDC, junior idle 20k): with an observation between exit and buy the bill is the no-buyer control (0.20 USDC apart); without it junior pays 925.91 USDC more than the control (1,851.44 vs 925.53). Closed by the senior receive-shares gate (a later task); until then the same L4 trust in the hourly keeper applies.
Also: any senior holder can redeem, call `observeSenior()`, and redeposit in one transaction, permanently lowering the minimum and with it the bill (or the carry) by their stake times the loss; the other holders bear it. The same works at the settle itself through `_exposed(supply)`, and `observeSenior()` is permissionless, so a flash-sourced position does it for shares the caller never owned if senior shares ever become flash-sourceable. The senior receive-shares gate (a later task) closes it.

## L11. Felix bad debt: a gated exposure snapshot; junior pays for a deficit Felix may later recover
Vault V2 never marks a Felix (MetaMorpho v1.1) bad debt: it stays inside Felix's `totalAssets` as `lostAssets`, Felix's share price does not move, and senior's gross real price (what the baselines use) does not show it. The cover adds a Felix term, `(lostAssets now - lostBase) * fraction`, scaled to the exposed senior shares over the senior supply at the exposure snapshot, built from two parts with different owners. (1) `lostBase` per Felix vault: a scalar that flows cannot move, re-taken at every baseline reset (constructor, end of each settle's cover, a successful `syncSenior()`). (2) The EXPOSURE: for each senior adapter that fronts a Felix vault, `fraction` = the adapter's Felix shares over the vault's share supply (PRICE_UNIT precision), the adapter's assets (for the dust guard), and senior's supply. It is re-taken ONLY by `snapFelixExposure()`, callable by the agent or the owner and never by a permissionless call (`syncSenior`, `deposit`, `settle`, `observeSenior` never touch it), because it is the one input a flash deposit into Felix or senior can steer. `snapFelixExposure()` forgives nothing while the vault stays listed and probes: a vault already in the snapshot keeps its `lostBase`, only a vault new to the snapshot starts at its current `lostAssets`, and an adapter that is gone or fails a probe at the snapshot drops out together with its `lostBase` (item 8). The only live read at the settle is `lostAssets` (it only grows in v1.1 and moves on a real loss); a failed probe counts as 0 and nothing reverts. Probes are low-level, capped at 50k gas, and read exactly one 32-byte word.
What this guarantees: flash deposits into Felix or senior wrapped around the permissionless calls (a sync, a junior deposit, a settle, each the last reset before the loss), third-party Felix withdrawals and deposits after the loss, and the settle itself cannot move the Felix fraction; a paid deficit is re-based and never comes back; a falling Felix ratio never credits. Between snapshots, senior exits and entries do not move the fraction either, but they scale the bill through `exposed` (see L10); AT a snapshot the fraction and the supply are read live (item 3).
What it does not guarantee:
1. Age. Senior exits between snapshots scale the Felix bill by the senior supply left, not by senior's share of Felix, which shrinks less because Felix's own supply shrinks too: with half of senior gone the bill was 19,537 USDC against a true pro rata share of 22,161 (an under-bill of about 12% on that loss, until the next snapshot). The exposure is as old as the keeper's last `snapFelixExposure()` call (staleness = keeper cadence, hourly; the keeper call arrives with a later task, 5/8). The `lostBase` is older than that whenever a sync is refused: while a carried debt is outstanding, while a Felix loss above the dust guard is booked and unbilled, or while senior's price is below the baseline; a settle then re-bases it. Run `snapFelixExposure()` once after senior is funded: the constructor's snapshot is whatever senior looks like at deployment, and it is empty (fraction 0, bills nothing) if junior is deployed before senior holds Felix shares; this depends on the deploy order.
2. Allocator, both directions after the snapshot. Out of Felix: senior holds nothing there, but the snapshot still bills `fraction at snapshot * delta lost` (over-bill; measured: senior fully out of Felix, bill 34,352 USDC, equal to the bound). Into Felix, or a zero fraction at the snapshot: under-bill, senior absorbs the loss (measured: snapshot at fraction about 0, billed 0, senior's pro rata share afterwards 48,902). Both end at the next `snapFelixExposure()`.
3. Third parties and the trusted key can steer the snapshot's timing. `snapFelixExposure()` reads live values at a predictable (hourly) time. A third party can deposit `d` into senior or Felix and hold it across one keeper snapshot, or sandwich the keeper's transaction (flash-sourced, in the same block), which inflates `felixExpSupply` or lowers `fraction`: later Felix bills shrink by about `S / (S + d)` (deposit into senior, `S` = senior supply) or `T / (T + d)` (routed to Felix, `T` = Felix assets). It is worst AFTER a loss is booked: `syncSenior()` refuses then, but the keeper keeps snapshotting until the settle bills it. The agent or the owner can do the same at its own snapshot, and a leaked agent key can. Bound: the Felix term itself (at most senior's whole pro rata share of one loss, fraction to 0); a third party pays only the cost of holding `d` across the snapshot, or the flash fee. The optional guard (`snapFelixExposure` does nothing while a booked, unbilled Felix loss is above the dust guard, the same rule as `syncSenior`) is NOT in: it takes the runtime to 24,586 B, 10 B over the 24,576 limit. Mitigation until it fits: the keeper must not call `snapFelixExposure()` while a Felix loss is booked and unbilled (keeper rule, 5/8); the settle then bills on the last clean exposure.
4. A loss Felix has not booked misses that settle's NAV: `lostAssets` is a stored value, updated on Felix's next touch (any deposit, withdraw or accrual by anyone). A junior exiter priced at that settle escapes its share of the loss and the next settle bills it to the stayers. Mitigation: the keeper touches Felix right before the settle (5/8).
5. The dust guard: `syncSenior()` refuses to reset while any entry's booked loss exceeds 1 ppm of the adapter's reference assets (the same guard as the price baseline); smaller bumps, which Felix books by rounding, never block a sync and are forgiven at the next reset. A real loss split into sub-1 ppm bookings that syncs keep forgiving costs whoever engineers it real bad debt (each piece is a real loss to Felix) and gains nothing: the forgiven part is below 1 ppm of senior's assets.
6. Another steering path, in the real loss and not in the bill: a flash round trip through Felix (a deposit and a redeem walk Felix's queues) can change which markets hold Felix's funds, hence Felix's own share of a later bad debt, in either direction. Measured on the fork: a 10M round trip before the bad debt moved the booked loss on a 1M senior from a bill of 48,888 USDC to 34,352. The bill follows the real loss (it equals the independent pro rata figure of that world); it is not steered away from it. Documented, not fixed.
7. Junior pays for a deficit Felix may later recover or other depositors may bear, and is not repaid; a recovery lifts senior's gross price above the baseline, so nothing is billed twice. `coverInProgress()` reads 0 for a Felix cover (the loss was never marked). Junior's OWN Felix position is still valued gross in `vaultAssets` (Low, unchanged). That is also why a Felix bad debt does not move L12's ratio: see L12, "What it does not see".
8. Entries that leave the snapshot forgive their loss, and new adapters wait for a snapshot. An adapter removed from senior, or one whose probe fails during a `snapFelixExposure()`, is popped with its `lostBase`, so a booked, unbilled loss on that vault is forgiven; on re-add the vault starts at its current `lostAssets`. A Felix adapter added to senior before any agent snapshot is not billed until a snapshot runs. All three are curator or agent actions (or a Felix that stops answering) and are not covered by the exposure gate.
Measured (real borrow and real `Morpho.liquidate`, oracle assumed down 99%): about 990,000 USDC of bad debt in Felix's largest USDC market, 1M senior; owed 48,888 USDC against an independent pro rata figure of 48,880 (shares over supply, read from the chain). Items 2 and 6 each measured 34,352 USDC in different scenarios (allocator fully out of Felix: bill 34,351.77 equals the bound; flash round trip through Felix before the loss: bill 34,351.77 against an independent figure of 34,346.32 for that world), the same number because a deallocation and a round trip both reshuffle Felix's markets the same way here.

## L12. A priced exit is paid pro rata when junior's holdings fall short of the reserve; claims need a provable solvency
After a settle prices an exit, its USDC is earmarked in `reservedAssets` but stays wherever the agent put it. A venue loss between pricing and claim can leave junior's holdings (idle USDC plus every position, net of the carried cover debt `carryOwed()`) below the reserve. Before 2/8 the first claimant was paid in full from idle and the next was refused whole (`InsufficientLiquidity`) until someone topped the contract up; and while holdings sat below the reserve a newcomer's deposit minted at the stale frozen price and filled the hole with their own USDC (audit E2(e)).

**What `claimExit` does.** If idle USDC alone covers `reservedAssets + carryOwed()` it pays `shares * price` exactly as before (no valuation). Otherwise it values junior: `ratio = min(1, max(0, holdings - carryOwed) / reservedAssets)` and pays `shares * price * ratio`, debiting the reserve by the FULL claim. Each claim leaves the ratio unchanged (holdings and reserve fall in proportion), so every claimant of the same shortfall takes the same percentage loss whoever claims first; a donation or a recovered venue only raises the ratio, capped at 1, and the sum of payouts never exceeds holdings. The carried debt is subtracted once, in the same net figure the NAV uses. A claim worth 0 is paid 0 without a valuation.

**Claim order decides WHEN, not HOW MUCH.** Payment is all-or-nothing on liquidity: the payout must be in idle USDC (`InsufficientLiquidity` until it is). The ratio counts every position, but only idle can pay. Measured by the audit's bank run: junior holds 15.27 USDC of senior fee shares the keeper never recalls; ratio 0.9428; the first claimant is paid 1,422.46, the second is owed the same but idle is 1,407.18, so it reverts until the shares are redeemed (test `test_E2_e_ratioCountsPositionsButPaymentIsIdleOnly`). The amount each gets does not depend on order; the time does. Liveness therefore depends on the keeper/agent recalling or redeeming EVERY position, including junior's senior fee shares and every Felix and Morpho position, whenever idle < `reservedAssets + carryOwed()` (keeper rule, fixes 5/8). L5 still applies.

**Deposits after a shortfall.** `deposit` reverts `Insolvent()` while holdings net of the carried debt are at or below `reservedAssets` (at equality NAV is 0 and the frozen price is stale; a junior with nothing reserved and nothing held is not refused here, it fails on `ZeroPrice`). Once claims drain the reserve that check stops firing, so the first claim paid at a ratio below 1 also sets `depositsPaused` (event `DepositsPaused(true)`): deposits stay off until a settle has re-frozen the price and the owner unpauses. A claim paid at ratio 1 never pauses.

**Failure mode, stated precisely.** A claim fails with `VenueBroken` only when junior cannot prove it is solvent: a venue view reverts AND the readable holdings (idle plus every readable position, net of the carry) are below `reservedAssets`. That is the E2(e) case itself with an unreadable venue; if the readable part already covers the reserve the claim is paid in full (true holdings are at least the readable part). The owner's `forceRemoveMarket` / `forceRemoveVault` unblocks it by dropping the broken venue from the valuation (the claim then pays at the ratio of what is readable); they also pause deposits (A7) and the owner unpauses after re-listing.

**What it does not see.** (1) The ratio is read at claim time, not fixed at pricing: a claim made before a loss is paid in full, one made after shares the loss, a recovery after a claim does not reach it. (2) A loss the venue has not marked. MetaMorpho v1.1 books bad debt as `lostAssets` and leaves its share price unchanged; junior's own Felix shares stay at gross value in `vaultAssets` (L11 item 7), so a Felix bad debt does not lower holdings, the ratio stays 1, early claimants are paid in full and the hole falls on the stayers or the last claimants. Unrealised Morpho Blue bad debt behaves the same way (supply assets are valued at the last accrual). Netting junior's Felix entry by its share of `lostAssets` would cost about 150 B of JuniorVault bytecode and two more staticcalls per valuation; it is not implemented, and bytes are not the main reason. Netting junior's own Felix shares by `lostAssets / totalAssets` would make junior's NAV depend on a live ratio that Felix flows move: a flash deposit into Felix changes `totalAssets` for one transaction, so it would move junior's NAV at deposit, claim and settle time, a pricing lever for anyone. That is the flow-steerable input fixes 1/8 removed from the C9 cover (the exposure is re-taken only by the gated `snapFelixExposure()`). A robust version needs a gated snapshot of the same kind (C9 v4); that is a follow-up. (3) Junior's stayers are wiped out first: NAV 0, price 0 at the next settle, and until then deposits are refused (`Insolvent`/`Paused`/`ZeroPrice`) before any exiter takes a haircut. (4) A claim that lands a wei or two under the reserve through rounding while stayers hold nothing also counts as a shortfall and pauses deposits.

## L13. Removing a venue clears junior's USDC allowance only
`removeVault`/`forceRemoveVault` zero the USDC allowance to that vault; `removeMarket`/`forceRemoveMarket` zero Morpho's only when the last market goes (Morpho serves every market, so zeroing it earlier would break the markets that remain until the agent re-approves). Any other token the agent approved to a vault survives its removal, and `setStakeToken` zeroes the stake token's allowance only for the vaults still listed (`revokeAllowances` walks the list). A token approved to a vault that was removed BEFORE that token became the stake token keeps its allowance, and once it is the stake token the agent can no longer call it (`execute` refuses the stake token as a target), so the allowance cannot be revoked by the agent; the owner has no function for it. Reaching it takes the owner listing a hostile vault, approving the future stake token to it through an approval rule and removing it before `setStakeToken`; zeroing the stake token in `_dropVault` would not help, because the stake token is not known then. Mitigation: the owner sets the stake token before the first vault approval of that token and never writes an approval rule for it.

## L14. FeeRouter sweeps: the band bounds the drift, not the price; a snipe needs the keeper's flush (3/8)
Owner order for a swap token: `setRouter`, `setFloor`, then `seedPrice(token, price)` from a source the owner checked (seeding before `setFloor` fails closed: a new oracle clears the seed). Until seeded, `sweep` reverts `PriceNotSeeded`. `sweep` refuses when the oracle is more than `maxPriceMoveBps` (10%) from the last accepted price and more than `maxCumulativeMoveBps` (30%) from the owner's last seed, either way. The drift compounds: accepted steps can add up to the cumulative bound, and a manipulation inside the band is not refused (measured: two ~9% sandwiched sweeps paid recipients 8,984.73 and 8,190.01 USDC for 10,000 T; an outsider ratcheting 9% under the reference stopped at the cumulative bound after 3 sweeps, 5,308.81 USDC lost against the seed price on 30,000 T). A sweep moves the reference only when its oracle value is at least `maxSweepUsdc / 100`, so dust cannot walk it. A real move beyond the bound stalls sweeps until the owner calls `seedPrice`. The floor oracle must not be movable by the keeper or by the swap venue (never the spot of the swap pool); ORPH's only market is the seeded v4 pool, so it needs an oracle that does not read that pool. `setFloor` assumes an immutable oracle (re-seed after a proxy or feed change behind the same address). `maxSweepUsdc` bounds the USDC that comes out of one sweep (oracle value of `amountIn` and the measured USDC), not the value of the tokens sold; positive slippage at the cap is refused, so the keeper sizes sweeps below the cap. The keeper key still takes at most `maxSlippageBps` of `min(balance, maxSweepUsdc)` per sweep through an aggregator-style router (audit F3(a), unchanged). A positive rebase on a parked token makes the excess unparked (sellable); a negative one can leave the balance under `totalParked` (unparked 0, audit F5(e)).

`distribute()` is permissionless. Alone that does not stop a fee snipe on junior: an attacker can deposit into junior and flush the router's USDC in one transaction, minting shares before the fees are in NAV. The bound is the keeper calling `distribute()` every tick (fixes 5/8): the gain is at most one tick's fees x junior's share (25%) x the attacker's pool share. Without that flush the window is unbounded in time.

## L15. The senior receive-shares gate refuses honest deposits and transfers in during a loss or cover window (4/8)
`SeniorReceiveGate` (set by the senior curator, 3-day timelock; wiring is 7/8) closes the recognition lag (C7), the exit-then-buy residual (C3b senior side) and the late buyer (C3). It refuses NEW senior shares (deposit, mint, transfer in; redemptions are never gated) to every account except junior, the senior adapters and the owner's allowlist while ANY of: (1) senior's gross real price is below junior's expected price (a loss is outstanding, until the settle covers it); (2) junior carries unpaid cover (`carryPerShare != 0`); (3) a booked Felix loss is not yet billed (an entry's `(lostAssets - lostBase) x fraction` is above 1 ppm of its reference assets, junior's own guard from `_felixOwed`, so the gate and junior always agree on what counts as a loss; rounding-sized bumps that anyone can book with dust do not refuse); (4) `coverInProgress()` is above `maxUnrecognised` (default 10 USDC, owner-settable up to 1,000).

**UX cost.** After a loss, depositors are refused until junior's settle (up to 7 days) and then until the cover is recognised: about 2 to 4 days per cover (L1: a 2% cover takes 3.7 days to recognise at the live maxRate, which is 0.548% of senior a day). A FeeRouter push of about 12 USDC on a 420k senior is above the 10 USDC threshold for 90 seconds (measured), then the gate reopens; pushes under 10 USDC never refuse. Existing holders are unaffected and can always redeem.

**Grief cost.** Anyone can donate USDC to senior to hold the gate shut: a donation of D keeps it shut for D / (senior size x 200% a year) of the excess above 10 USDC, about 22.8 USDC an hour on 100k and 96 USDC an hour on 420k (computed from the 200% a year maxRate). The donor loses D to the holders (a gift), so the attack costs the attacker what it blocks and is bounded by the recognition rate; it blocks deposits only, never redemptions.  The Felix term is not cheaply griefable: a bump under 1 ppm is ignored, and a bump above it is a loss junior bills at its next settle (measured guard: a `lostAssets` bump of 3.3 USDC at the fork's 100k senior). A carry is a real state.

**Gas.** A full check costs about 577k gas cold (two sweeps of the adapters' `realAssets`), 3.5k for junior, 5.7k allowlisted, 11.8k an adapter or a fee recipient; a senior deposit costs about 905k without the gate and 1.20M with it, inside HyperEVM's 2M small block. Felix's `previewRedeem` loops its withdraw queue (10 markets at the fork: 349k gas cold, about 35k per market) and the check pays it about twice, so the cost grows with the queue. The check runs in a self-call capped at 1.0M gas: BEYOND THE CAP THE GATE FAILS CLOSED (a starved call refuses; measured: at a 400k limit the answer is `false`, at 1.5M `true`), so transfers in would refuse and deposits would need a big block. Re-measure after any change to Felix's queue or to the adapter set; leave the cap above the check and the check plus a deposit under 2M.

**Fail-closed lockout.** Every failed read refuses newcomers. A Felix vault whose `lostAssets` probe starts failing (an upgrade, a paused or broken vault) therefore locks newcomers out until the owner snaps the exposure (`snapFelixExposure`, which drops an entry whose probe fails) or the curator removes the adapter. Exits are unaffected. The gate also follows junior's exposure snapshot: with the construction-time snapshot taken before any funds (`fraction` 0), a Felix loss neither bills nor refuses until the keeper's first `snapFelixExposure()` (L11 item 8).

**Not covered.** Redemptions, junior-side exits, a Felix bad debt that nobody has booked yet (L11 item 4) and unrealised Morpho Blue bad debt behind the market adapter (it reaches senior's price only when a market books it) are outside the gate. A loss smaller than 1 ppm of senior never reads as a loss (the same guard as junior).

## L16. Launch scripts: what the launcher does not stop (6/8)
`SeedLauncher` removes the pool-init block (O1): a pre-initialised key can no longer stop the seed, only make it dearer for the attacker. Residuals, all pre-launch and all on the pool's own value:
1. **Public pre-signal.** The deploy of the launcher and the `ORPH.approve(launcher, 600M)` are public transactions before the seed. An attacker who sees them can pre-initialise the key and park liquidity in the way; the seed is atomic, so it cannot be blocked for free. With the default cap 0 the first run reverts and the seeder re-runs with a cap. Blocking costs the attacker gas and locked capital only: a blocked seed reverts and the attacker's position stays withdrawable; its HYPE is spent only when the seed succeeds. The approval stays: it is to the launcher, which only moves the ORPH inside `seed`.
2. **The team sells some ORPH before the sale opens, at about the launch price.** Up to the cap. Measured: liquidity straddling the opening tick took 18.57M ORPH at an average FDV of $4,961 (the launch price minus the 1% fee, which accrues to the attacker's own position); a badly placed position took 25.52M at $5,893. So the attacker does not pay a premium in general; the buyer of that ORPH gets it at the launch price and the pool opens with correspondingly less ORPH of liquidity (the position holds the remainder).
3. **The cap is the seeder's call, and the gap's tick count is a cost.** A cap far above what the team accepts lets a gap position take more ORPH early; a cap too low blocks the seed until re-run. There is no on-chain judgement of a fair amount and no price floor (a floor would reopen the free block). The re-price crosses every initialised tick: about 31k gas each (462k with none, 830k with 10 dust ranges, 1.29M with 25, 2.05M with 50, 3.58M with 100). An attacker that spreads dust positions can push the seed past a small block (2M) with roughly 50 ranges (2,054,300 gas measured at 50); seed in a big block whenever the gap holds more than about 45 initialised ranges, and if it would exceed 30M, pre-cross with ordinary capped swaps first (not atomic).
4. **Initialised above the plan with ORPH liquidity in the gap** (it needs ORPH, which only the four recipients hold before launch) reverts `PriceBlockedAbove`; an empty gap passes.
5. **Not an anti-sniper.** The launcher does nothing about buyers after the mint; the pool is open from the moment the position exists.
6. The refund goes to the caller: 1 wei of HYPE sent to the launcher makes a contract caller without a `receive` revert (`RefundFailed`); the planned caller is an EOA (the deployer).
