## Circuit facts
Circuit trades tokens and tokenized stocks on supported chains, perpetuals on Hyperliquid, Lighter and Lighter Robinhood, perps, spot, options and vaults on Derive, real-money perpetuals on Papertrade (papertrade.xyz, unrelated to Circuit's simulated paper trading), prediction markets on Polymarket, spot on Kraken, Coinbase, OKX, Binance and Bybit through connected exchange API keys, prediction markets on Kalshi through a connected Kalshi API key, and perpetuals on stocks, indices, FX and commodities on QFEX through a QFEX account Circuit creates for one of the user's EVM wallets. Agents are public programs built from written descriptions that trade on a schedule or signal and can be paper traded with simulated funds before real money. Circuit also supports lending, Metric and Uniswap v3 and v4 liquidity. Circuit charges no fees today; gas, venue, routing and bridge fees may apply.
## Venue facts
Orders: `amount` is `{ size }` in base units (shares for outcomes, contracts for options) or `{ value }` notional in the market's quote asset, never margin. A leverage multiple sizes notional once; a close or reduction is never multiplied. On Hyperliquid and Lighter a perpOpen's `margin` sets cross or isolated at a leverage up to the market's maximum (the maximum when omitted) before the order, as its own operation run on the same approval; a market holding an open position keeps its margin mode until that position closes. Without `margin`, the order trades at the account's current setting; only when it would not fit there does Circuit first raise a cross market to its maximum leverage. A market set isolated, or one that takes only isolated margin, is never changed without `margin`. Market reads own minimums, size steps, price ticks, leverage caps and margin rules. A null rule is one the venue does not set. On Hyperliquid and Lighter, a market or limit perp entry can attach take profit and stop loss as one venue order: exits take a market entry's fill or activate once a resting entry fills completely; canceling a partly filled entry cancels its exits and leaves the filled part unprotected. Exits on an existing position are separate reduce-only trigger orders. Stop ends a session's execution without closing its positions, liquidity or resting orders, which the user closes by asking Circuit; pending market/ioc orders fill or cancel and never rest.

Hyperliquid trades on HyperCore, including wrapped majors in spot, which uses spot orders, never swaps. A unified or portfolio-margin account's one USDC balance funds perps, swaps and transfers; a never-configured (`default`) or standard account keeps spot and each perp DEX apart. A swap into HyperCore USDC opens a missing account. Lighter spot trades from its spot balance, separate from perps. Lighter Robinhood is Lighter's separate Robinhood Chain deployment with its own markets and USDG margin; its spot trades from unified USDG collateral, with no spot/perp transfer. A swap into either Lighter deployment's dollar opens its account; Lighter Robinhood's first deposit comes from Robinhood Chain USDG. "Robinhood" means Robinhood Chain.

Derive vault deposits/withdrawals are queued. A wallet has one subaccount per risk universe (`PRIME` BTC and ETH, `MIDCAP` HYPE, `ALT` other altcoins, `RWA` XAUT); collateral is `UNIVERSE.ASSET` on network `derive` and margins only that universe. Swaps into collateral such as `PRIME.USDC` deposit from any supported network in one operation; swaps between universes move collateral. Derive takes no leverage setting and attaches no take-profit or stop-loss to an entry; trigger orders are separate.

Papertrade's market read supplies paused actions and minimum margin, notional and stake, maximum notional and leverage, and per-side headroom; unreadable rules are unknown, never guessed. Paused actions and undersized stakes are refused. Each open is a separate isolated position; there are no resting orders, partial closes or added margin. Deposits swap dollars from any network straight to the wallet's Papertrade deposit address, opening its account; withdrawals go only to the same wallet's HyperCore USDC. Each way moves at least 10 USDC; the first deposit pays a one-time 1 USDC HyperCore activation fee. Withdrawals spend only live available balance, leaving about 1 USDC; open margin and queued winnings are unavailable until positions close and payouts arrive. Earned PAPER stakes/unstakes by exact amount; claiming credits USDC rewards to Papertrade USDC.

Connected exchange pairs use the exchange's own base/quote asset codes; Kraken calls bitcoin XBT and dogecoin XDG. A buy's `"max"` value or sell's `"max"` size uses live available trading balance net of fees, not funds reserved by orders.

Kalshi contracts pay 1 USD when their outcome happens and nothing otherwise, and Kalshi settles held contracts into the account's USD itself, so nothing is redeemed. Kalshi accounts are funded on kalshi.com; Circuit neither deposits nor withdraws. Every Kalshi order is a limit on a contract pair priced in USD per contract, an ioc at the ask buying now; Kalshi nets the two outcomes of a market, so a buy is refused while the other outcome is held, and a sell is limited to the contracts held.

QFEX trades perpetuals on stocks, indices, FX, commodities and pre-IPO companies with USDC margin from a QFEX account Circuit creates for one of the user's EVM wallets under Wallets; every QFEX order is a limit order (market orders are ioc limits within the slippage); each order uses the leverage the QFEX account already holds for that market, which Circuit never changes. Circuit deposits USDC on Arbitrum One to a QFEX account and withdraws USDC from it to the account's registered Arbitrum One address, one withdrawal at a time; these accounts have no QFEX login, so nothing happens on qfex.com.

Polymarket market orders fill completely or not at all; limit orders rest as gtc or maker-only alo, optionally expiring, until filled, canceled or expired. The Deposit Wallet trades with pUSD, funded by pUSD or USDC.e on Polygon, and sends/swaps only those two; the engine converts USDC.e to pUSD and sets allowances. Positions can sit in any EVM wallet. Only redeemable resolved markets can be redeemed, once per market per wallet; redemption covers both outcomes, and a market whose held shares all lost is not redeemable: they stay held at no cost. A session's inventory is its own record, not proof that the wallet still holds shares another session redeemed. pUSD is the `kind: "token"` holding on `ethereum:137`, so outcome prices are per share in dollars.

Lending supplies, borrows, withdraws, repays and changes collateral or categories on Aave V3 and V4, Spark, Euler, Morpho and Kamino through lending operations, not raw transactions. Swaps may cross networks and routing chooses the bridge; sends stay on their network. Simulation shows a raw call would succeed now, not that its contract is the intended one.

Metric liquidity is supported only on Ethereum, BNB Chain, and Robinhood Chain, in green-tier pools: only green pools are sure to have no withdrawal extension blocking exits. Bins are priced around an oracle reference and their widths vary, so a range is percents from the market price, rounded outward to whole bins, and an open refuses a range wider than the pool's bins span; trading fees compound into bins, with nothing to claim. A position is one pool and salt across its bins, and Circuit values every bin in the session's value and PnL. Adds are bounded to the slippage around the market price; removals take no onchain minimum and return the bins' current, oracle-priced balances.

Uniswap v3 liquidity uses existing pools of the canonical v3 factory, ERC-20 tokens only, at the factory's fee tiers; Uniswap v4 liquidity uses existing hookless, static-fee pools; both only on Circuit networks with verified registry deployments, and in v4 native and wrapped currencies are distinct. Circuit values NFT principal and uncollected trading fees separately, without assuming APY or unclaimed external incentives. Going out of range changes token exposure and stops earning trading fees until back in range; it never protects principal. A range spanning the price needs both pool tokens, and an open budgeting only one is refused; a one-token range must clear the price by more than its slippage, such as -0.1 to -0.02 at 0.01 slippage on Metric or 1.2 to 3 at 1 slippage on Uniswap, and starts out of range, which a rebalance rule must allow for. An agent session starts with one token and top-ups add only that token, so an agent whose range spans the price must swap part of it before each open; one waiting for the other token never opens. The user chooses between that swap and a range on one side of the price. Users manage Metric and Uniswap positions through reviewed liquidity proposals in Ask and agents through agent.liquidity, never raw transactions; both use live simulation, passkey authority and separately reviewed token allowances sized to the approved budget. Stop never closes liquidity; it offers one reviewed removal of the session's share of each position, returning both pool tokens and their fees to the wallet without swapping.

Elysium (`ethereum:99901`, HYPE gas) is Kinetiq's chain settling to HyperEVM; Relay bridges HYPE and USDC in and out like any other network. Montra (montramarkets.xyz) is Elysium's token launchpad.

Testing: backtests replay only Hyperliquid, Lighter and Lighter Robinhood perpetuals; other money actions and outside requests fail a backtest. Neither paper sessions nor backtests run raw transactions. Paper sessions open, add to and remove Metric and Uniswap liquidity at live pool state without moving the pool; paper Uniswap positions value principal only, without trading fees. Paper Polymarket orders pass the live order checks; market and immediate-or-cancel orders take the live book within their limit, market orders whole or not at all, resting limits fill when recorded Polymarket trades cross them, and paper orders expire at their expiresAt like live ones.
## How Circuit runs an agent
An agent is one exported function in `index.ts`: `run(agent)`. Circuit calls it each time a trigger declared in `circuit.toml` fires, and `agent.trigger` says which one; choose the trigger that matches when the strategy acts rather than polling for an event a trigger already reports. A session runs one run at a time and queues triggered runs behind it. Any user can start the agent on their own wallets, so the code reads `agent.walletAddress` at run time.

Every run starts from nothing. `agent.allocation` is the truth about what the session holds, read fresh each run and again before each money write resolves, so it already includes what the run just did; filter its `holdings` by `kind` (`token`, `supplied`, `borrowed`, `perp` or `prediction`). Only the session's own orders and liquidations change those holdings, never the user's manual trades on the same account. `agent.memory` is the only state that survives between runs and belongs to this session; keep in it only facts the rule needs across runs, such as a high-water mark or a copied position's id, under stable keys; keep a session under 1,000 keys: rewrite or delete keys instead of adding one per run or per scan. The session pays its own network fees, so a gas token it was never allocated shows a negative quantity: a cost already paid. The first run acts: read the history a signal needs with a lookback instead of collecting it across runs.

A strategy that must react within a minute or hold state between decisions is continuous: set `continuous = true`, declare no schedule, and loop on `agent.next({ until })`, such as `until: Date.now() + 60_000`: choose a wait no shorter than the venue's price refresh (Hyperliquid fifteen seconds, Lighter one minute). It returns a fired trigger or `null` at `until` and refreshes `agent.allocation` (except after another wallet's `balanceChange`, which leaves it unchanged); outside continuous mode it throws. Circuit restarts every continuous process together from inside `agent.next()`, so the code needs no restart handling; it delivers each fired trigger once, and loses a trigger whose run fails after receiving it.

## Principles
**Code the user's rule, and make it able to trade.** A rule the user did not state is a way the agent silently never trades: on Circuit, sessions that ran for days without one order had a gate, threshold, warm-up, freshness check, cooldown or "evidence" requirement nobody asked for, or a dry-run switch. So the agent adds no condition, exit, stop, limit, band or filter the user did not state, and a strategy the user leaves to you uses a simple signal that fires within days on current data, never one that waits for a rare event. Implement what was asked: only a venue the SDK cannot write to or a credential the agent cannot hold blocks it, and fabricated signals, monitor-only modes, manual input or simulated trading are not substitutes. When the user asks to be told about something, `agent.log` at that moment is the requested behaviour, and a message the user did not ask for is an unrequested behaviour. Before finishing, read the code as its first run on today's data with exactly `minimumAmount` allocated; name the order it places or the user's condition not yet met (if neither, something unrequested stops it), and check every principle below against the code.

**Decide from what you read this run.** Agents that remembered a position instead of reading it closed positions that were already gone, reduced a short they thought was a long, redeemed markets with nothing to redeem, and stacked new hedges on old ones. Each decision reads the state it acts on in the same run: allocation for holdings, `orders.listOpen({ venue })` for resting orders, the venue's account read for positions, `operations.pending()` for actions still settling, and a market's order rules from its market read. Read a venue account once per decision, not on every wake.

**Give every value a meaning before you use it.** The most common code failure is arithmetic on something that was not there: `rows[0].price` on an empty page, a decimal helper handed `undefined`, `Number()` of an unset setting turning into `NaN` so the agent never trades. A read that succeeds and returns nothing is an answer: no candle yet, no market listed, nothing to claim. Decide what that answer means for the rule, usually "the condition is not met: log it and return", and write that branch. A null `priceUsd` or mark, a position or holding that is not there, and an amount below the market's minimum are such answers, never a reason to throw: an agent that throws on one fails identically on every run until the user stops it. Circuit owns freshness and completeness, so data is used as served, without age, gap or row-count checks of your own. A read that fails, an order that is refused or an outside request that errors throws out of `run` by itself: Circuit records the failed run with its full error, shows it to the user, flags the session after repeated failures and runs the next trigger as usual. So the code has no try/catch, fallback value, retry, skip-and-log or flag that turns a failure into a quiet run, except three kinds: a `MoneyActionError` whose `code` is `VENUE_PAUSED`, `OPEN_INTEREST_CAP` or `SLIPPAGE_EXCEEDED` (the price moved past the order's limit before it matched), a refusal that changes nothing; `NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE` on a copied buy or on a deposit into a vault at its cap; and a failure the user named; for those, log what happened and return or retry later as the user said.

**Build each order inside the venue's rules.** Venues refuse an order below the market's minimum, off its step or tick, above its leverage cap, in a margin mode it refuses or with a trigger on the wrong side of the mark, and the refusal repeats identically on every later run: agents that hard-coded a minimum, checked it before rounding or sold a share of a shrinking balance failed thousands of runs in a row. So the code reads each market's rules in the run that orders, from the read that names the market, and ships no venue number of its own. `agent.data.markets` rows (Hyperliquid, Lighter and Derive), `exchanges.markets.list({ exchange })` and `derive.options.list()` carry `minimumNotional` (order value in the quote token), `minimumSize` (base units), `sizeStep` and `priceTick`, and perpetual rows also `maximumLeverage` and `marginRule`; `polymarket.markets.retrieve({ asset })` carries `minimumNotional` for a market buy's `value`, fees included, and `minimumSize` and `priceTick` for a limit order; Papertrade's `markets.list()` carries its own limits; and a swap's dollar value reaches `MINIMUM_SWAP_USD` from `circuit:sdk`. The engine rounds a size down to `sizeStep`, so an order meets `minimumNotional` only when its rounded size times the price it trades at does: pass `{ value }` at or above it and the engine keeps it there, or size with `decimalAlign(decimalDivideCeil(minimumNotional, price, 18), sizeStep, true)`. Circuit trades every perp at its market's `maximumLeverage`, cross wherever the market allows it, so a leverage multiple only sizes notional and no order sets leverage. Size from `agent.allocation`: the allocation the user chose is everything the agent trades, never held back in part, and the starting minimum is a floor, never a size; a size the user does not state splits it evenly across the positions held at once, a repeating buy spreads it across its stated period, and a leverage multiple makes notional the allocation times the multiple. A size below the market's minimum rises to it when the session can fund it, and otherwise the agent logs once that it waits for funds and returns; a leftover below the minimum, such as the rest of a partial fill, is never an order. Buy sizes leave a sellable holding, and an exit that no route quotes or that sits below the minimum is caught once, logged, recorded in `agent.memory` under the asset and not attempted again until that holding's quantity changes. Check these rules still hold after the session loses half its allocation. Market orders fill; choose `alo`, `ioc`, FOK-style or a limit price only when the strategy calls for that behaviour.

**Do money math in decimal strings.** Every amount the SDK takes or returns is a decimal string of whole tokens, and floats or `Number()` silently lose precision on them. Use the `circuit:sdk` decimal helpers for all money math, cross from a float signal with `toFixed`, and truncate long products toward zero with `decimalDivide(x, "1", 18)`; USD fields carry at most 18 decimals, quantities their token's own. `"max"`, `{ balancePercent }` and `perpReduce` measure the session's own holding, and the engine rounds spends down and minimums up to each token's smallest unit, so the code writes no parsers or rounding of its own. Selling all of a network's gas token (ETH, SOL, HYPE, POL) uses `"max"`, which leaves the network fee; its exact held quantity cannot also pay that fee.

**Pay for everything the action costs.** Orders failed for insufficient margin, for gas or rent the wallet lacked, and for fees taken on top of the amount sent. A position costs its spot notional plus its perp notional divided by the market's `maximumLeverage`, both from one balance when the venue's collateral is unified, so a hedged pair costs both legs. Margin never leaves the collateral token holding: agents that sized every new order from that holding's whole quantity kept opening until the venue refused each order for insufficient margin, so a new order's margin comes from the collateral holding's `availableQuantity`, which already leaves out the margin and unrealized losses of the session's open perps, and below the order's margin the agent logs that it waits for margin and returns. Leave room for fees and gas the allocation pays.

**Name each decision with `once`.** Agents that rebuilt their own dedupe from keys, flags and history bought twice, skipped buys after one failure, or failed every run after it. A money write's second argument `{ once, per? }` names the decision, such as `{ once: "buy", per: "week" }` or ``{ once: `copy/${positionId}` }``: `per` (`hour`, `day` or `week`, UTC) scopes it to the current period. A write whose `once` already succeeded returns that operation without preparing or submitting again; while one is still settling, it throws `MoneyActionError` with outcome `unresolved`; a failed one frees the name, so a later run decides again from live balances. Give each asset or leg its own name, and use `once` for every action that must not double, closes and hedges included.

**Let the engine do the money mechanics.** The engine owns preparation, prerequisites, quotes, balance and minimum checks, routing, approvals, submission and settlement; the strategy owns only the decision, so it calls `create` directly, even for "best price", and quotes only when the rule needs a number to decide. A money write returns the completed operation or throws `MoneyActionError` (`outcome.status` `rejected`, `failed` or `unresolved`), which the code lets throw; code that catches any Circuit error rethrows that same error, never a new `Error(message)`, so Circuit's and venues' temporary failures keep their code and are not reported as agent bugs. Previews, quotes and plans are never persisted, and past `error` operations are history. What an order paid is its result's `averageFillPrice` (fills' size-weighted price before fees; null or missing when nothing filled); `limitPrice` is only the worst price accepted. Close a position with a plain opposite order for its exact session quantity. A holding's `entryPriceUsd`, `priceUsd` and `unrealizedPnlUsd` are null when unknown, never zero; realized results, fees and funding already land in the session's collateral holding, and a user's limit those numbers cannot measure uses the nearest one they can. Allocation is a budget, not an authorization, so the agent's own arithmetic keeps every order inside it.

**Live within the runtime.** Circuit kills a continuous agent that does not return to `agent.next()` within minutes of a restart request, so every loop iteration ends in `agent.next()` and no await inside it waits longer than the work needs. Read only the data the rule needs: market reads across every market hit the statement time limit. A failed read or refused action is never retried on a timer. Circuit refuses a session's 13th new Papertrade action within a minute. Time periodic work such as staking on the wall clock at a per-session offset, for example when `Math.floor((Date.now() + agent.sessionId * 60_000) / 1_800_000)` changes, never from process start. Backtests answer every `agent.data` read as of the simulated clock, replay continuous agents on the simulated clock, and use the starting asset's venue; Venue facts say what backtests replay, and paper sessions simulate only what Venue facts and the venue sections say they do.

**Circuit data first.** `agent.data` is the first source for any asset, market or venue fact. `agent.data.prices({ network, asset, interval, bars })` names an asset exactly as its allocation holding does (`{ network: "hypercore", asset: "BTC" }`, a token's contract on its network) and answers its newest `price` and exactly `bars` gap-free bars, oldest first, or `bars: null` while the asset has less history than that, which is the rule's "not enough history yet" branch; search while building and ship the exact address, symbol or key you found, never a search whose first row could change. A venue's own API (Hyperliquid info or exchange, Lighter, Kraken, Papertrade, the Polymarket CLOB or Gamma) never replaces account, order, fill or market-rule state the SDK serves: paper accounts exist only inside Circuit, direct calls bypass its egress, and venues change formats without notice. An outside source is for a fact Circuit lacks, needs no credentials or paid plan to keep answering, is named in DESCRIPTION.md, answers on HTTPS port 443 or HTTP port 80 (other ports, private addresses and raw UDP are refused) with the shape the code reads, serves the right chain id when it is a named RPC endpoint, and is called with `AbortSignal.timeout`. Protocols the SDK does not model are reachable through `agent.wallet.transactions`; an outside API serves only a fact Circuit data lacks, never a balance, position or account state.

**Write the least code that does the job.** A rule stated in a few sentences is a few dozen lines: read what the rule needs, decide, place the order. No state machines, wrappers, helpers or validation of values you wrote yourself. Type SDK argument objects where they are built so discriminants stay literal, await SDK calls, and read past terms from an operation's typed `action` (null if unparseable).

## A complete agent
The user asked: "Go long BTC with leverage while it's up on the day, otherwise stay out." `circuit.toml` declares an hourly `schedule` trigger aligned `fixed`, a HyperCore USDC `[startingAsset]` (`network = "hypercore"`, `asset = "0"`) with `minimumAmount = "2"`, and an integer `leverage` setting defaulting to 5 between 5 and 20. Notice what each line of the code answers: the empty-data branch, the state read before deciding, the close without a key, the market's minimum read with its price, free collateral, decimal math and one key per hour.

```typescript
import { decimalCompare, decimalMultiply, type RunFunction } from "circuit:sdk";

export const run: RunFunction = async (agent) => {
  const { price, bars } = await agent.data.prices({ network: "hypercore", asset: "BTC", interval: "1h", bars: 24 });
  if (!price || !bars) return agent.log("BTC has no day of history yet; deciding again next hour.");
  const rising = decimalCompare(price, bars[0].open) > 0;
  const position = agent.allocation.holdings.find(
    (holding) => holding.kind === "perp" && holding.asset === "BTC",
  );
  const long = position !== undefined && decimalCompare(position.quantity, "0") > 0;
  if (rising === long) return agent.log(`BTC is ${rising ? "up" : "not up"} on the day; holding.`);
  if (long) {
    await agent.log("BTC is no longer up on the day; closing the long.");
    await agent.hyperliquid.orders.create({ kind: "perpReduce", asset: "BTC", amount: { size: "max" } });
    return;
  }
  const usdc = agent.allocation.holdings.find(
    (holding) => holding.network === "hypercore" && holding.asset === "0",
  );
  const { rows: [market] } = await agent.data.markets({ venue: "hyperliquid", venueSymbol: ["BTC"] });
  if (!market) return agent.log("Hyperliquid lists no BTC market; deciding again next hour.");
  const free = usdc?.kind === "token" ? usdc.availableQuantity : "0";
  const notional = decimalMultiply(free, String(agent.settings.leverage));
  if (market.minimumNotional && decimalCompare(notional, market.minimumNotional) < 0)
    return agent.log(`Waiting for funds: ${notional} USDC of BTC is under its ${market.minimumNotional} USDC minimum.`);
  await agent.log("BTC is up on the day; going long.");
  await agent.hyperliquid.orders.create(
    { kind: "perpOpen", asset: "BTC", side: "long", amount: { value: notional } },
    { once: "open-btc", per: "hour" },
  );
};
```

`minimumAmount` is 2 because 2 USDC at the lowest allowed 5x reaches the 10 USDC `minimumNotional` the BTC row showed while building.

## Settings and configuration
Prefer good defaults to settings. Declare at most three, each a choice a person must make before starting (size, the main signal threshold), and read every one in code through the generated `circuit-settings.d.ts` without a cast. A number the user specifies is a default, written once in `circuit.toml`, and each number setting has a `min` and `max` where the strategy still makes sense. A default is a working value: a placeholder such as `yourname/main` or the zero address starts sessions that do the wrong thing. Each setting's `description` is one plain sentence, in DESCRIPTION.md's everyday words, saying what a higher or lower value does for the user; never code words such as true, false, cap_weighted or virtual allocation.

Declared triggers are the ones the code expects. `[startingAsset]` names the exact asset the strategy starts from, never the whole wallet, on the venue it trades (HyperCore for Hyperliquid, Polygon for Polymarket). Its `minimumAmount` is the floor Start enforces: the least allocation at which the code's own sizing (its split, leverage and percentages) puts every order it holds or places at once at or above its market's minimums as the market read shows them, plus the fees and gas it pays from the allocation, rounded up only to the token's precision. A padded or round number locks out users the strategy could serve, and a low one starts sessions that can never trade. Preserve `agentId`.

## What the user sees
`agent.log` is the user's window into why the agent acts: what the rule saw and decided, reports and status, and agents cannot send push notifications. Every order, fill and close already shows as its own activity row with its amounts, and a write whose `once` already succeeded returns that operation again, so a log never restates a trade; it gives the reason, written before the write it explains. Write short plain sentences with symbols, formatted amounts and percentages, such as "ETH crossed above its 20-day average; buying.", and leave addresses, hashes, raw units and transaction details to the transaction cards. Summarize a loop with one line per kind of outcome and its count. `agent.log(message, { error: true })` is for a failure the user should act on; a wait or skip the rule expects, such as a full market or a missing price, is a plain log written once when it starts, with `agent.memory` remembering it, rather than again every run.

## Orders
Every venue's `orders.create` takes one shape per kind of market. A perp takes `{ kind: "perpOpen", asset, side: "long" | "short", amount, takeProfit?, stopLoss? }` or `{ kind: "perpReduce", asset, expectedSide?, amount }`; a spot pair, an exchange pair or an outcome (Hyperliquid `#…`, Polymarket token id) takes `{ kind: "spotBuy" | "spotSell", asset, amount }`; each takes an optional `execution` and `message`. `asset` is the market exactly as its allocation holding names it: the perp coin, the outcome token id, or the venue's spot pair. `perpReduce` and `spotSell` sizes and Hyperliquid and exchange `spotBuy` values may be `"max"`. Omit `execution` for a market order within 1% of the mid; the engine reads the mid, sets the limit and rounds size to the market's decimals. Pass `{ kind: "limit", price, timeInForce }` only for a price the strategy chose; `"ioc"` cancels what does not cross, `"gtc"` rests until filled or cancelled, and `"alo"` rests as maker only and is rejected if it would cross. On perps and Kraken, `{ kind: "takeProfit" | "stopLoss", triggerPrice }` executes at market once the mark (Kraken: the last trade) crosses it, and `{ kind: "takeProfitLimit" | "stopLossLimit", triggerPrice, price }` then rests as a limit at `price`; a buy's stop loss triggers above the mark (a breakout entry or a short's stop) and a sell's below. Triggers on `perpReduce` are reduce-only. For attached exits, `perpOpen` takes `takeProfit` and `stopLoss`, each `{ triggerPrice, price? }`. A stop the strategy chooses goes on its entry this way, so the position is never unprotected, and sits clear of the level the entry condition crosses: an exit at that level fills within hours and the next run buys back, paying fees and slippage on every round trip. The exits are reduce-only, open orders of their own linked by `entryOperationId` (and by `entryOrderId` while the entry rests). To manage an entry that has not filled, read it, cancel it, and place a new entry with new exits. If Hyperliquid itself cancels a partly filled resting entry for insufficient margin, it places the exits at the entry's full size. Close the session's position with `{ kind: "perpReduce", asset, amount: { size: "max" } }`.

Every venue returns the same order: `orderId`, `asset`, `side`, `size`, `filled`, `limitPrice`, `averageFillPrice`, `state`, `type`, `triggerPrice` and `reduceOnly`. `agent.orders.listOpen({ venue })` lists the session's resting orders on that venue, TP/SL included, and the venue's `orders.cancel({ orderId })` cancels one, returning `{ orderId, state }`. A placed or listed order's `triggerPrice` and `limitPrice` are the prices the engine rounded to the venue's precision, never the strategy's own: identify an order by the `orderId` `create` returned or by repeating its `once` write, never by recomputing its price, and cancel every order you replace.

## Hyperliquid
`agent.data.markets` has the market list and each market's order rules. Pass `venueSymbol` from `agent.data.markets({ venue: "hyperliquid", assetType: "perpetual" })` or `asset` from `spotMarkets.list()` unchanged; it is case-sensitive. Main perps look like `BTC`, builder-dex perps keep their prefix (`xyz:NVDA`), and spot pairs are `@107` or `PURR/USDC`. The SDK exposes no order book depth, account leverage or maximum trade size, or prices fresher than the venue's mid refresh. A market's `minimumNotional` is in its quote token at the price the order trades, a buy's at the ask and a sell's at the bid; closing a whole position is exempt. An agent may place about fifteen orders a minute across a user's sessions; Circuit refuses orders over that, and each refusal fails its run. Paper sessions fill Hyperliquid outcome (HIP-4) orders (assets start with `#`) immediate-or-cancel on markets that settle on a perp price at a set time, and settle held shares on Circuit's recorded 1-minute close of that perp at that time; they refuse resting outcome orders and outcome markets that settle any other way, and backtests refuse outcome markets. A perp holding's collateral figures are in the collateral token, USDC when `collateralAsset` is "0"; `priceUsd` is that mark in USD, null when the collateral is not a dollar.

## Lighter
Pass `venueSymbol` from `agent.data.markets({ venue: "lighter", assetType: "perpetual" })` unchanged, such as `ETH`, or a spot pair such as `ETH/USDC`. `"gtc"` and `"alo"` limits rest for at most 28 days. The engine refuses a resting or trigger order below the market's `minimumSize` and a resting limit below its `minimumNotional`; market and `"ioc"` orders, closes included, carry no minimum. Standard accounts pay no trading fees; funding is paid hourly. `agent.lighterRobinhood` takes the same calls as `agent.lighter`; markets come from `agent.data.markets({ venue: "lighterRobinhood" })` (stock and ETF perpetuals such as `NVDA`, spot pairs such as `NVDA/USDG`); `routeTransfer` is refused, and `value` and `amountUsdc` count USDG. Its funding asset is `lighter-robinhood`, asset `3`.

## Derive
Derive perps are named by base currency such as `ETH` and spot pairs such as `WSTETH-USDC`; `optionBuy` and `optionSell` name an instrument such as `ETH-20261225-4000-C`. `agent.derive.options.list({ currency, expiry })` lists the option chain and quotes one expiry with greeks. ## Papertrade
`agent.papertrade.positions.open({ asset, side, amount: { value }, leverage })` opens one isolated position on a market Papertrade lists, at Hyperliquid's mid; `value` is its notional and its margin is value ÷ leverage. `agent.papertrade.markets.list()` is the only source of Papertrade's rules: never fetch exchange.papertrade.xyz. Each entry gives a market's `paused` actions, `minimumMargin`, `minimumNotional`, `maximumNotional`, `maximumLeverage`, `minimumStake`, per-side `headroom`, and the `impact`, `winFeeRate` and `bustBuffer` that price wins and liquidations; read it in the same run before acting, and size every open within its limits. Each open has its own `positionId`; a pair's legs open together with `Promise.all`. An open can use the session's Papertrade USDC holding minus every Papertrade perp holding's `marginUsedInCollateralHolding` and the margin already committed this run; `fundFrom: "queued"` takes margin from winnings queued for payout instead. `positions.open` returns the shared venue order, its `orderId` the new `positionId` and its `averageFillPrice` the entry; `positions.close({ positionIds })` closes up to twelve at once and returns one reduce-only order per position at its exit price, and `positions.closed({ positionIds })` returns how each named position that has closed ended: `exitPrice`, `pnl`, `paperMinted` and `liquidated`. `account.retrieve()` reads the wallet's live Papertrade account: available `balance`, `queued` winnings, every open position on the wallet with ids, margin and liquidation prices, and `paper` with unstaked `balance`, `staked` and claimable USDC `rewards`; claim only when `rewards` is worth it. Losses pay nothing beyond the loss, and a liquidation forfeits the whole margin. The funding asset is `papertrade`, asset `0`. `paused` is keyed by Papertrade's action names (`openPosition`, `closePositions`, `paperStake`, `paperUnstake`, `paperClaim`, `registerDeposit`, `withdrawToCore`, `registerSessionKey`); skip an action whose entry is not false, which Papertrade refuses with `VENUE_PAUSED`. `headroom` is the largest notional each side accepts now, and a larger open fails with `OPEN_INTEREST_CAP`: shrink the margin to fit when the shrunk open still meets the minimums, and otherwise skip that leg and log that the side is full. A null fact is unknown and its market's `errors` says why: never act on it; open the healthy markets' legs, then throw its first error. Headroom shrinks as others open, so `OPEN_INTEREST_CAP` can still arrive: log it and retry on a later run. Papertrade queues each intent, so an open can wait minutes before it fills. `agent.operations.cancel({ operationId })` withdraws an open, a one-position close or a staking call still in that queue; when one leg of a pair is refused, find the other in `operations.pending()`, cancel it, and close it only if it filled first.

Decide from the session's Papertrade perp holdings, one per open position with its `positionId`, `liquidationPrice` and margin, together with pending operations, so a late-settling open never doubles a leg. Close each leg with its own `positions.close`, losing legs first: one close naming several positions fails whole when any of them already closed. A liquidation or a close on papertrade.xyz ends a holding as the session's own close, its result already in the session's Papertrade USDC and PAPER holdings. Winnings pay from the pool that losses refill, and a win the pool cannot cover waits in `queued`. Use `paper.stake({ amount })`, `paper.unstake({ amount })` and `paper.claim({})` for staking and rewards; staking and unstaking are instant. The session's PAPER is a token holding (`ethereum:999`, the PAPER contract): what its own closes minted minus what it staked, which a `papertrade` token holding `STAKED_PAPER` carries. Stake only an amount at or above `markets.list()`'s `minimumStake`, never a remembered number; a smaller stake is refused. Trade first and stake, unstake or claim last, so a refused staking call fails the run after its trades. To copy a Papertrade trader, a `perpPositionChange` trigger on their wallet reports each of their positions opening or closing by `positionId` with side, margin and leverage; name each copy ``once: `copy/${positionId}` ``, and close it when the trader's position closes.

## Polymarket
Never match question or title text or hard-code a resolution time. Ship a single event's `slug`; search each period for a recurring one. Retrieve the event by `slug`, choose the market by structured fields (`endDate`, `groupItemTitle`, `line`, `sportsMarketType`, `gameStartTime`) and the outcome by `outcomes[].name`. Search returns at most 20 active events, which shorter markets can fill on a busy subject. For a recurring market, choose the event whose `endDate` falls on the period's last day: `endDate` can mark that day rather than the closing instant, and `startDate` is a listing time that can fall days into the period. If the choice is not unique, throw naming the candidates. An order needs the outcome's token id, `outcomes[i].asset`; outcome order is not fixed, so match on `name`. A held outcome carries it as `holding.asset`, a trigger as `agent.trigger.asset`.

Use `{ kind: "spotBuy", asset, amount: { value } }` or `{ kind: "spotSell", asset, amount: { size } }` for a market order. A buy's `value` is the most pUSD it spends, Polymarket taker fees included, so pass the full stake; it must reach the market's `minimumNotional` from `markets.retrieve({ asset })`, 1 pUSD after the market's taker fee, which Circuit refuses with `BELOW_MINIMUM` before anything is sent. `orders.quote` returns `null` when the book cannot fill the order (no sellers for a buy, no buyers or too little depth for a sell): that is the answer "no liquidity right now", not an error. A limit order takes `amount: { size }` and `execution: { kind: "limit", price, timeInForce, expiresAt? }`, and buys or sells exactly `size` shares (two decimals) at `price` (on the market's `priceTick`) or better; `expiresAt` makes a resting order end by itself at that instant (at least 2 minutes ahead, such as a game's `gameStartTime`). A resting order needs at least the market's `minimumSize` shares; a cancel returns once Polymarket reports it canceled or filled. Resting buys reserve their pUSD.

Declare a `resolution` trigger for venue `polymarket` with no asset; it fires once per resolved market the session holds, with `agent.trigger` carrying one held `asset` and the winning `outcome`. `isRedeemable`, true only for a held outcome that pays, is the other signal, and a losing market needs no redeem; skip quoting or selling a held outcome whose `acceptingOrders` is false. Event and screen reads list only markets open to buys; before buying any other asset, such as one copied from another wallet's trades or remembered from an earlier run, read `markets.retrieve({ asset })` and skip it unless `acceptingOrders` is true and `resolving` is false: once a UMA resolution is proposed, Circuit refuses every buy into the market with `NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE` and quotes it as `null`, while sells still work. Never poll for resolution. `agent.polymarket.positions.redeem({ asset })` takes one asset from the market; call it once per `conditionId`, in sequence. A market the wallet no longer holds completes with an empty `redeemed`; a market that has not resolved yet is refused. The result lists shares redeemed, not pUSD received.

## Kalshi
`agent.kalshi.events.search({ query })`, `({ seriesTicker })` or `({ eventTicker })` returns matching series, a series' open events, or one event's markets, whose `yes` and `no` each carry the pair id (`KALSHI:<ticker>.YES/USD`) that `agent.kalshi.orders.create` trades and the best `bid` and `ask` in USD per contract; ship one series or event ticker and choose markets by structured fields. Orders are limits: `{ kind: "spotBuy" | "spotSell", asset, amount: { size }, execution: { kind: "limit", price, timeInForce } }`, size in contracts at 0.01 steps, an ioc at the ask to fill now. A held contract sells only up to the contracts held; Kalshi settles resolved contracts into USD itself.

## Lending
`agent.lending.markets.list({ network, protocol? })` returns `{ markets, unavailable }`: pick a row's `protocol`, `market` and `asset` from `markets` (with `canSupply`, `canBorrow`, `supplyApy`, `borrowApy`, `loanToValue`), and treat a venue in `unavailable` as unreadable this run, never as offering nothing. `agent.lending.positions.supply({ network, protocol, market, asset, amount })` and `.borrow(...)` open from a row; `.withdraw({ network, position, amount })` and `.repay({ network, position, amount, payWith? })` reduce a held position, where `position` is the `supplied` or `borrowed` holding's `asset` in `agent.allocation`. `amount` is whole tokens, `{ balancePercent }`, `{ loanToValue }` or `"max"`; for an agent `"max"` is the session's own holding and closes it fully. A `borrowed` holding's `quantity` and `underlyingQuantity` are negative; read its `loanToValue` for the account's loan-to-value rather than dividing quantities. Repay before withdrawing collateral that secures a loan. Circuit adds approvals, Euler and Kamino account setup and the Aave V4 collateral switch on a first supply itself.

## Merkl rewards
`agent.rewards.list({ network? })` returns the wallet's claimable Merkl rewards as last indexed, one row per `network` and reward `token`, with the paid `asset`, `symbol` and `claimable`. `agent.rewards.claim({ network, tokens? })` claims the listed `token`s, or everything claimable on the network, and is refused when nothing is claimable. Claimed tokens join the session that claimed them; unclaimed rewards are not `agent.allocation` holdings.

## Copying wallets
FOMO (fomo.family) traders trade across EVM networks and Solana without bridging: a cross-network trade is the payment leaving one trader wallet and the bought token arriving in another seconds to minutes later, and their `balanceChange` runs can arrive in either order. FOMO perpetuals trade on Hyperliquid, including Trade[XYZ] markets, from the trader's EVM wallet, reported by `perpPositionChange`, which also watches that wallet's Lighter main account and Papertrade positions and names each change's venue. Anyone can send tokens to a trader's wallets, so Circuit leaves out spam and every received Solana token that is frozen or that is unlisted with a live freeze or mint authority, PermanentDelegate or TransferHook, in `balanceChange` triggers and `agent.solana.transactions.list` alike; a transaction left with nothing never fires, and agent code needs no token checks of its own. `agent.fomo.traders.retrieve` resolves a username, and a continuous agent follows a changing set of traders, such as a leaderboard's top 100, with a `balanceChange` or `perpPositionChange` trigger that names no wallet and `agent.watchedWallets.set` refreshed from `agent.fomo.leaderboards.retrieve`; both triggers share the one list, and a perp trader the user picks in a setting is watched the same way. A token a trader buys can have no route yet, such as a launchpad token still on its bonding curve: catch `NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE` on that copy's `create`, log it once and skip the copy. A copied sell of a share whose dollar value is under `MINIMUM_SWAP_USD`, and a copied buy the session's dollars can no longer fund at that minimum, are skipped before quoting, never sent: traders sell in many transactions, so a share of a small holding is otherwise refused with `BELOW_MINIMUM` on every one of them.

## Montra launches (Elysium)
Montra (montramarkets.xyz) launches tokens on Elysium (`ethereum:99901`) straight into their own Uniswap v4 pool against native HYPE: there is no bonding curve or migration, and graduation is only a price line about 17x the opening price. `GET https://api.montramarkets.xyz/api/tokens?stage=` (`new`, `graduating` from 50% progress, `graduated`; keyless, paged by `page`) lists tokens with `address`, `price` (HYPE per token), `priceUsd`, `marketCapUsd`, `holders`, `traders1h`, `volume1hUsd`, `launchedAt` (ms), `creator`, `totalFeePips` and `graduation.progressBps`. Trade with `agent.wallet.transactions.create` calling Montra's LaunchRouter `0xc6e9a8e1a99023b67067b8a3605403b8d7b3a157`: `swapExactIn((PoolKey key, bool zeroForOne, uint128 amountIn, uint128 minAmountOut, address recipient, uint256 deadline))`, where every HYPE pool's `key` is `(0x0000000000000000000000000000000000000000, token, 0x800000, 200, 0x6b1216a40b65c83e9c9e18a562c446697b8cf8ec)`. A buy is `zeroForOne: true` sending `amountIn` HYPE as value; a sell is `false` with no value, after approving the router for the token. Set `minAmountOut` from `price` less the fee and slippage. Every trade pays the token's `totalFeePips` (1% to 10%), and a buy inside its anti-snipe window, up to 30 minutes after `launchedAt`, pays up to 99%, so buy only tokens launched over 30 minutes ago. KyberSwap does not route Montra tokens yet.

## Swaps, vaults and contracts
`create` simulates eligible providers and takes the highest output whose guaranteed minimum meets the floor: expected output less slippage, or a higher `minimumReceive`; execution preserves it. Pass no `engines` or `slippage` the user did not name, and never pin a quote's provider: estimates are unsimulated, and a pin removes alternatives. Same-chain EVM routes (KyberSwap, Velora, Metric, Uniswap) report no venue fees, CoW Swap reports its execution fee in the source token and delivers after a solver fill, `venuePriceImpact` only on some Uniswap routes, and gas as `amountUsd` alone; a missing cost field is unreported, not zero and no reason to wait, and the quoted output already reflects price impact. Leave `minimumReceive` out unless the strategy has a price the trade must beat. Circuit refuses a swap worth under `MINIMUM_SWAP_USD` from `circuit:sdk` with `BELOW_MINIMUM`, so a swap sized as a share of a shrinking balance compares its dollar value with it first and logs once that it waits for funds below it. `quote` and `create` take the same request; `amount: "max"` or `{ balancePercent }` sizes from the session's own holding, never more than the wallet can spend after fees. The engine adds swap approvals itself. Wrapping is a swap to the destination token. A completed swap's result carries the `received` amount, and `agent.allocation` already includes it.

Where the network has the `erc4626` swap provider (`swapProviders` in the network facts), a deposit is a swap from the underlying to the vault's share token and a withdrawal is the reverse, with approval and minimum output handled; never hand-build `deposit` or `redeem` there. A vault at its deposit cap refuses a larger deposit with `NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE` naming the room left, so a strategy choosing among vaults moves on to the next-best one. Elsewhere, approve the vault for the underlying, then deposit, as two raw transactions. Arc (`ethereum:5042`) has no `erc4626` provider; USDC is its native dollar and gas token. Its ERC-20 view of the same balance never appears in allocation: treat the native entry as the USDC balance and never add the two.

`agent.wallet.transactions.create({ network, request })` simulates before admission; `preview` is optional inspection with no authority. The engine approves only its own swaps, so a raw contract's allowance is its own keyed transaction first. Write every address all lowercase or in its exact EIP-55 checksum: Circuit and viem refuse a mixed-case address whose checksum is wrong, as some venue docs print.

Circuit serves every balance, position, supply, collateral, loan, loan-to-value and health fact through `agent.allocation`, the only source for what the session holds; code never reads a balance or position from an RPC endpoint or a venue API. The wallet can hold positions other sessions or the user opened, and those never belong to this session. A raw transaction's inputs (contract addresses, market parameters, reserve accounts) are constants found while building, never reads that decide what to do.
- A vault share balance is the allocation holding whose `asset` is the vault address, marked at net asset value.
- Any asset's price and history, held or not, is `agent.data.prices`; never value holdings with swap quotes: thin-route price impact looks like a loss.
- `agent.data.yields({ depositToken: [{ network, address: vault }] })`, keyed on the vault, never the underlying, gives APY with and without rewards, TVL, `withdrawalOutcome` and `withdrawableAmount`.
- `agent.transactions.list()` pages what the session's past operations moved, oldest first, on hosted targets only: `{ rows, nextCursor }`, or `for await` over it for every movement.

### Liquidity
Metric, Uniswap v3 and Uniswap v4 positions go through `agent.liquidity.positions.change({ network, action })`, never raw calls: `open` (module, pool, `range: { lowerPercent, upperPercent }` around the current price, budgets, slippagePercent), `add` (module, position, budgets, slippagePercent) or `remove` (module, position, percent; 100 closes). Circuit sizes within the budgets, derives each approval as its own operation and bounds opens and adds by `slippagePercent` (1 unless another tolerance is agreed); Uniswap removals carry 1% floors and collect fees. Ship a Metric pool from `GET https://api.metric.xyz/public/v1/evm/{chainId}/metadata?tier=green`, taking only its `poolAddress`, a Uniswap v3 pool as `{ token0, token1, fee }` from an `agent.data.pools` uniswap-v3 row (ERC-20 tokens only, so WETH, never the native coin) and a Uniswap v4 pool key from `agent.data.pools`. A Metric open's range must fit the pool's live `span` from `agent.liquidity.pools.retrieve({ network, module: "metric", pool: { pool } })` (read context kind=metric while building), so read it before each open and log instead of opening when the briefed range does not fit; a briefed range that does not fit today is the user's choice to change, never narrowed in code. Size budgets in whole tokens from session token allocation. A position is several `supplied` rows sharing one `position`: act once per distinct `position`, passing that value to add or remove; `percent` is of what the session holds, so 100 removes exactly the session's share. Its holdings' `range` gives its live offsets from the pool's price and `inRange`, so decide from them, never from a saved price or a mark, and treat a null `range` as unread this run; a one-sided range is out of range from its open, so implement its briefed trigger as a distance past an edge, never `inRange` or an offset's sign alone. Pass a briefed range's percents unchanged, never converted to bins or ticks, and implement the brief's plan for getting the second pool token (Venue facts); without one, ask the user before editing, since waiting for the token is no plan. Rebalance is separate exit, conversion and entry operations, each keyed by the position it replaces (such as `open/<removed position>`, `open/first` before any), never by a time window: save the removed position in `agent.memory` before reopening and key the open from it, so an open refused this run and retried in a later run keeps its key instead of replaying an earlier open. Stop offers the user a reviewed removal of the liquidity the session still holds, so build no unwind setting.
